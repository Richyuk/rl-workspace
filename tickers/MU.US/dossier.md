## Summary

Micron is one of three scaled DRAM makers, with Samsung and SK hynix, and the third HBM supplier; it also makes NAND. FY2026 (to 3 Sep 2026) revenue was $133.2bn, up 256% at a 74.6% operating margin, almost all from price (DRAM ASP up about 180%, bits up in the mid-20s %). At the 6 Nov close of $690, 43% below the June peak, EV is 7.4 times trailing EBIT, but the reverse DCF needs 17.2% revenue growth a year for ten years with the margin fading to 18%, a rate at most 5% of 1,375 comparables achieved; held at today's margin the price implies shrinking revenue. The open question is margin durability: $134bn of take-or-pay obligations and AI demand against margins that ran from -37% to +75% in ten years and new supply from Samsung, CXMT and Micron itself.

## Business

Micron designs and makes DRAM (including HBM, DDR, LPDDR and GDDR), NAND (SSDs, managed NAND, multi-chip packages) and a little NOR in its own fabs in Taiwan, Singapore, Japan, the US, Malaysia, China and India, using subcontractors for some steps. DRAM was $100.7bn and NAND $31.8bn of FY2026 revenue (Note 16). Sales go direct to hyperscale and OEM customers, through distributors and, until the exit announced in December 2025, under the Crucial consumer brand. The 10-K says 1-gamma, the first EUV DRAM node, carried the majority of fourth-quarter DRAM bit output, HBM4 36GB 12-high is in volume production, HBM4E ramps in calendar 2027, and fabs are being added in Idaho (first wafers mid-2027), New York (2030), Hiroshima, Singapore and Tongluo, Taiwan (bought from PSMC for $1.8bn in March 2026). Since the third quarter of FY2026 it has signed multi-year take-or-pay 'strategic customer agreements' with price bands, backed by $12.75bn of customer deposits and $7bn of letters of credit; remaining performance obligations were about $134bn at year end, a quarter due within twelve months.

**Cyclicality.** Twenty years of annual history (first fiscal year 2007). Revenue fell in 7 of 19 years: 2009 -17.8%, 2012 -6.3%, 2015 -1.0%, 2016 -23.4%, 2019 -23.0%, 2020 -8.4% and 2023 -49.5%; the FY2023 trough had a -37.0% operating margin and a -9.1% gross margin. Operating margin ran from -37.0% (FY2023) to 74.6% (FY2026), against a previous peak of 49.3% (FY2018), and ROIC from -9.5% (FY2023) to 110.5% (FY2026). The 2007-2026 revenue CAGR is 18.1% and the 2016-2026 CAGR 26.8%, but both are lifted by FY2026, in which price (DRAM about +180%, NAND about +200%) did most of the work and bit shipments rose only in the mid-20% range. That is a shortage price, not volume growth. Management and outside sources (Counterpoint and TrendForce, via the cited pages) describe tight supply into 2027 and 2028 because large new capacity arrives late, and the strategic agreements are meant to cushion a downturn, but management estimates them at over 35% of revenue through 2030 (Motley Fool summary), so most sales still follow market prices. One outside analyst notes that price momentum peaked in Q1 2026 and that the absolute price peak could fall between Q4 2026 and Q3 2027 (Luminix); that is a view, not a fact.

## Moat: narrow

Industry structure and scale. DRAM is made by three large firms plus China's CXMT and YMTC; NAND by Samsung, SK hynix, Kioxia, Sandisk, Micron and YMTC (10-K competitive conditions). A leading-edge fab costs billions and years: Micron spent $30.7bn of capex in FY2026 and guides about $25bn in the first half of FY2027 alone. Technology: the first EUV DRAM node (1-gamma) is now the highest-volume node, HBM4 36GB 12-high is in volume production, and Micron has been granted over 62,000 patents. HBM is a three-supplier market that needs customer qualification and advanced packaging capacity; Micron's HBM revenue share rose from 5.8% in 2024 (IDC) to 18% in Q2 2026 (Counterpoint). Contracts: the new strategic agreements are take-or-pay with price bands, cash deposits and letters of credit, and the 10-K says margins even at floor pricing should be meaningfully above any past cycle's peak; if that holds it is a contractual protection memory has not had before. Balance sheet: net cash of about $41bn on the cache basis and a 110% ROIC in FY2026 give room to outspend weaker rivals. The map proposed no chokepoint for Micron (chokepoint_proposals is empty), so there is none to test.

**Against.** The returns record. Over 20 years the business earned its cost of capital only in upturns: ROIC was 1.0% in FY2016, -9.5% in FY2023 and 1.8% in FY2024, with a -37% operating margin in FY2023. A moat shows in through-cycle returns, and the cycle-average here is a commodity's. FY2026's 81% gross margin came from price, not cost or share: DRAM ASP up about 180% and NAND about 200% on bits up in the mid-20% range, in a market the 10-K says is short of supply because of AI demand. That is a shortage rent, and the 10-K itself warns that AI-driven demand may not be sustainable. Competitors are closing the gap where it matters: Samsung's HBM revenue share rose from 21% to 33% between Q1 and Q2 2026 while Micron's was 18% (Counterpoint, via Silicon Analysts), SK hynix is still first at 50%, and Samsung says it expects about 40% by Q4 (press release summary). CXMT is about 10% of DRAM revenue (Counterpoint, per industry reports) and state-backed, and press reports say it is advancing a denser 4F2 server DDR5; the 10-K names Chinese government support as a source of DRAM and NAND oversupply. The three-supplier structure has not stopped price collapses before (FY2019, FY2023). The strategic agreements are estimated at over 35% of revenue through 2030, which leaves most revenue exposed, and a fixed-volume contract is only as good as the buyer's balance sheet. Labour is a new weakness: Micron's Taiwan unions are demanding a share of profit, a 24-hour warning strike took place on 22 Oct 2026 and Bloomberg reports construction of Fab 11E has been halted, though Micron says Taiwan operations are normal.

## Management and capital allocation

Ten years (2017-2026): operating cash flow $192.0bn, capex $117.1bn, free cash flow $74.8bn, dividends $3.5bn, net buybacks $9.1bn and stock compensation $5.6bn. Of that free cash flow, $59.0bn (operating cash flow less capex) came in FY2026 alone; the nine years before produced $15.9bn against capex of about $86bn, so for most of the decade the business reinvested nearly everything it made and returned little. Payouts were below free cash flow by $62.2bn over the ten years. Spending followed the cycle: capex was cut in FY2023 to $7.7bn and raised to $15.9bn in FY2025 and $30.7bn in FY2026 (gross; $3.3bn of government incentives came back in FY2026), and buybacks were small and episodic ($2.7bn in FY2019, $2.4bn in FY2022, none in FY2025). In FY2026, with cash flowing, management repaid $10.0bn of debt (about $9.3bn cash for $8.8bn of principal, with a $510m loss on early redemption), took gross debt to $5.2bn including leases, bought 2.5m shares for $650m (about $260 a share), paid $0.53 a share of dividends and bought the Tongluo fab for $1.8bn. The board raised the repurchase ceiling to $35.16bn from 9 Dec 2026, but buybacks are limited by the CHIPS Act direct funding agreements, and management also plans about $25bn of net capex in the first half of FY2027, a $250bn US build plan (CNBC) and repayment of $12.75bn of customer deposits in 2029-2031. The record is mixed: the 2022-2023 capex ran into a downturn that took ROIC to -9.5%, while FY2026 capex looks well timed in hindsight; the test of this cycle is whether the Idaho, New York, Japan, Singapore and Taiwan fabs and any large buyback are done at the top of the price cycle. ## Financial quality

Cash conversion is strong on the reported numbers: operating cash flow of $89.7bn was 1.06 times net income of $85.0bn and the screen's accruals ratio is -3.4% (cash above earnings); the screen's trap checks (interest cover, cash conversion, receivables build, inventory build, heavy dilution, decelerating growth, late filing, auditor change, controls) are all no. Three things make FY2026 cash flow less clean than it looks. (1) Taxes: the provision was $14.8bn (a 14.8% rate) but cash taxes paid were $1.25bn; Singapore's Pillar Two top-up of $9.03bn largely offsets $11.22bn of tax-incentive benefit, income and other taxes payable rose from $0.6bn to $5.6bn and noncurrent liabilities added $9.6bn to operating cash flow (MD&A: mostly noncurrent income taxes payable), so a large tax bill is deferred, not avoided. (2) Working capital: receivables rose $25.2bn to $36.2bn on four-fold quarterly revenue growth (about two months of fourth-quarter revenue), inventory rose only $2.0bn, accounts payable and accruals added $8.7bn and other current liabilities $3.1bn, mainly 'consideration payable to customers for pricing adjustments' ($4.3bn at year end). Revenue is stated net of estimated price protection and returns, and PwC's one critical audit matter is exactly that estimate, so earnings depend on judgment about price adjustments in a market whose prices are moving fast. (3) Customer deposits of $12.75bn are financing inflows (not operating), repayable in 2029-2031 if customers meet volume minimums; they are cash in the bank and a debt-like claim at once. Free cash flow: the cache carries TTM free cash flow of $53.9bn, but operating cash flow less capex is $59.0bn ($62.3bn after $3.3bn of incentives); the cache's quarterly free cash flow for the February and August 2026 quarters does not equal operating cash flow less capex (the annual row and the 10-year sum do). The screen's 5.4% FCF yield uses the lower figure at the build-date market value. Audit: PwC has been the auditor since 1984 and gave unqualified opinions on the statements and on internal control over financial reporting on 9 Oct 2026; audit fees and their growth are not available (see gaps). Trailing figures equal the fiscal year (four quarters add to the 53-week FY2026), so the screen's MIXED_TTM flag does not point to a mixed basis here; the filed check shows no deviation.

## Expectations: stretched; cases usable with caveats

At the 6 Nov 2026 close of $690 (the valuation's price; the facts pack's market block carries the 30 Oct close of $884.74) the reverse DCF reads three ways. With the margin fading from 74.6% to the industry median of 18.0% over ten years (the Track B base case), the price implies 17.2% revenue growth a year for ten years, taking revenue from $133bn to about $652bn; at most 5% of 1,375 comparables (all industries, USD 50bn+, since Micron's own industry group has under 30) grew that fast. If the 74.6% margin is held for ten years the price implies revenue falling 4.2% a year; if the margin drops to 18.0% at once it implies 29.4% growth a year. The price is therefore a bet on the margin, not on volume. Micron's own record: 2016-2026 revenue CAGR 26.8% and 2007-2026 CAGR 18.1%, but the first starts at the FY2016 trough and ends in the FY2026 price spike, and revenue fell in 7 of 19 years. On trailing numbers EV is 7.4 times operating income against an own median of 14.1 times, a 25th percentile of 10.2 and a minimum of 6.2 (over about five cached years), and the price is 3.9 times consensus FY2027 EPS of $176.50 against FY2026 diluted EPS of $74.33; the price is 55.8% below the Street target of $1,562. The screen's mid-cycle EBITDA yield is only 2.5%, which says the stock is expensive on mid-cycle earnings. Consensus asks for far more than the base case's first year: revenue of $275.5bn in FY2027 (2.1 times FY2026) and $319.7bn in FY2028; the company guided $61.5bn for the November quarter against $54.2bn in the August quarter, so growth is already slowing sequentially (13% guided against 31% in the August quarter and 74% in the May quarter). The market is not pricing growth; it is pricing the chance that price-driven profit falls back. The shortage argument is that large new supply is not expected before 2027-2028 (Micron's own Idaho fab mid-2027) and long-term agreements cover part of 2027-2028 volumes; the cycle argument is that memory makers add supply when prices are high, and CXMT, Samsung and Micron's own fabs all do.

**What would change it.** Evidence that the margin is structural: November-quarter results due 16 Dec 2026 holding about 86% adjusted gross margin on $61.5bn revenue, 2027 HBM4 contract prices holding at or above 2026 levels, the strategic agreements growing beyond 35% of revenue with price floors above the old cycle peak, no concession in contract pricing for the first half of 2027, and bit growth accelerating (so growth comes from volume as well as price). Evidence against: contract price growth slowing to zero or negative (one source forecasts +0-5% for Q4 2026), DRAM spot prices flattening, hyperscaler capex cuts or data-centre build delays after the US elections, CXMT yield and capacity gains, Samsung HBM4 share gains, new fabs adding supply from 2027 and labour cost step-ups in Taiwan. A fall in the margin to the 46% of the base case's year 5 would still leave far more profit than in any year before FY2026; a fall to the own 10-year median of about 28% would leave roughly 37% of FY2026's operating income at the same revenue. The 10-year growth test itself will not move until the price or the margin assumption does.

**On the code-generated cases.** Usable with caveats. (1) The Judge's rule already blocks a zone: the high case's year-5 value is 3.57 times the base case's, above the 2.5 limit. (2) The base case's headline return depends entirely on the exit rule: 7.5% a year at the DCF exit of 4.5 times EBIT, 46.5% at the industry exit of 32.3 times, and about 27.8% if year 5 were valued at the own median of 14.1 times (approximate).
